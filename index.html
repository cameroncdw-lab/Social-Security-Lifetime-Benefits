Federal tax on SS benefits

The IRS taxes SS benefits based on your provisional income — your other income plus 50% of your SS benefit. Up to 85% of your benefit can be taxable.

0%
Single <$25K
Joint <$32K
50%
Single $25–34K
Joint $32–44K
85%
Single >$34K
Joint >$44K

These thresholds haven't been inflation-adjusted since 1994. Source: IRS Pub. 915; SmartAsset 2026.

Provisional income = other income + tax-exempt interest + 50% of annual SS benefit. Taxable portion × marginal rate = estimated annual SS tax.

Still working in your 60s? — Earnings test

If you claim SS before your Full Retirement Age (FRA) and continue working, SSA withholds $1 of benefits for every $2 you earn above the annual exempt amount. These withheld benefits are NOT lost — they are credited back as a higher monthly check once you reach FRA.

Enter values to see impact.

2026 exempt amount: $22,320/yr (before FRA year). In the year you reach FRA: $59,520/yr, withheld at $1 for every $3 over. At FRA the withholding stops completely. Source: SSA.gov
