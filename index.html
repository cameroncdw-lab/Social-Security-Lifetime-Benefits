Federal tax on SS benefits

The IRS taxes SS benefits based on your provisional income — your other income plus 50% of your SS benefit. Up to 85% of your benefit can be taxable.

How the earnings test works

If you claim SS before your Full Retirement Age (FRA) and keep working, SSA withholds $1 for every $2 you earn above the annual exempt amount ($22,320 in 2026). In the calendar year you reach FRA, the threshold rises to $59,520 and withholding drops to $1 per $3.

The withheld money is NOT lost. At FRA, SSA permanently recalculates your benefit upward to credit every month that was withheld — effectively pushing your "effective claim age" later. This tool shows exactly how much is withheld each year and what your adjusted monthly benefit becomes at FRA.

Source: SSA.gov retirement earnings test · 2026 exempt amounts
